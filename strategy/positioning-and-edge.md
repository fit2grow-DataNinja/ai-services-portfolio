# Positioning and competitive edge

Status: Agreed direction; differentiation remains to be validated

## Positioning

External specialist providing **AI business solutions and continuous enablement**. The client buys a reliable business outcome and continuing methodological/technical expertise, not merely an AI skill or an application.

## Internal substitution threat

Companies can create and share AI skills, instructions, supporting code and, where permissions and connectors allow, access common business data. Competent internal teams may reproduce many of the same capabilities. A shared repository alone does not guarantee user permissions or safe data access.

## Candidate sources of differentiation

1. Tested business methodology and consistent calculations.
2. Integration with actual business processes and data sources.
3. Traceable outputs, validation, auditability, and error handling.
4. Faster implementation and lower total ownership burden than internal alternatives.
5. Ongoing maintenance, support, employee enablement, and relevant improvement advice.

These are hypotheses to demonstrate, not automatic advantages of external software.

## Positioning test for each service

Why buy this rather than use (a) an internal shared AI skill or (b) an internally built integrated workflow? Compare cost, time-to-value, reliability, integration, risk, maintenance, and actual client capacity.

## Role boundaries

Client owns business objectives, data, approved business rules, and decisions. Provider acts as external methodology/implementation specialist and ongoing contact. Avoid undocumented provider lock-in; ensure handover and agreed ownership.
